PATIENT ADMISSION NOTE
Department: Intensive Care Unit (ICU)
Date: October 24, 2023
Time: 14:30

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
Shortness of breath and severe chest pain.

The patient is a 65-year-old male presenting to the emergency department with acute onset of crushing chest pain radiating to the left arm, accompanied by diaphoresis and severe dyspnea. Symptoms started approximately 2 hours prior to arrival while shoveling snow. Patient took one sublingual nitroglycerin without relief.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
1. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (diagnosed 10 years ago)
2. Essential Hypertension
3. Hyperlipidemia
4. History of smoking (quit 5 years ago, 20 pack-year history)

CURRENT MEDICATIONS:
- Metformin 500mg PO twice daily
- Lisinopril 10mg PO daily
- Atorvastatin 40mg PO at bedtime

ALLERGIES:
Penicillin (causes severe hives and anaphylaxis)

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
- Vitals: BP 165/95, HR 110 (tachycardic), RR 24, Temp 98.6F, SpO2 92% on room air.
- General: Patient appears in acute distress, diaphoretic and clutching chest.
- Cardiovascular: Tachycardic, regular rhythm. No murmurs, rubs, or gallops.
- Respiratory: Tachypneic. Bilateral basal crackles noted on auscultation.

DIAGNOSTIC DATA:
- ECG: ST-segment elevation in leads V1-V4 (Anterior STEMI).
- Troponin I: Elevated at 2.4 ng/mL.
- Chest X-ray: Mild pulmonary congestion, no cardiomegaly.

ASSESSMENT:
1. Acute Anterior ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI).
2. Acute Hypoxemic Respiratory Failure secondary to mild pulmonary edema.
3. Uncontrolled Hypertension.

PLAN:
1. Activate Cath Lab for emergent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).
2. Administer Aspirin 324mg chewed stat.
3. Start Heparin drip per ACS protocol.
4. Administer high-flow oxygen via nasal cannula to maintain SpO2 > 94%.
5. Consult Cardiology immediately.
6. Transfer to Cardiac ICU post-procedure.
